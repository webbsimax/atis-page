Nicaragua

Jun 2016 · 2016-06-17

After an unsuccessful sloth hunt and having spent a lot more money than anticipated in pricey Costa Rica it was time to flee to cheaper pastures—Nicaragua. We'd carefully researched and planned our route from the Monteverde cloud forest to San Juan del Sur, at the bottom of Nicaragua. It involved getting a local bus down a bumpy dirt road, picking up locals as we went; getting off on an intersection at the InterAmerican highway; flagging down a coach; crossing the border; disembarking in a gateway town and finally catching a cab to San Juan del Sur. Easy, and doable in just a few hours, or so we thought

The first part of that plan went well, we got all necessary buses, got off on the highway and boarded our coach to the border. And that's where our troubles began. Just short of the entry to immigration at the border our coach stopped...and wouldn't move again for several hours. A number of illegal African immigrants, stuck in a battle between USA, Costa Rica and Nicaragua, were blocking the border and we couldn't pass. Several hours later we were allowed in, along with several hundred others who had backed up behind us waiting to get into Nicaragua. And so began the chaos of shouting, lines, officials, stamps and half arsed bag checks. There is no stress like being harassed by vendors, losing your bus and negotiating taxi fares all the while having no idea where your passport is. Anyway, we survived and found some backpackers to share a taxi straight to San Juan del Sur Nicaragua.

San Juan del Sur is predominately a backpacker town known for its surfing and apparently world famous Sunday Funday pool piss up. We'd not come for those things, rather because we had tracked down Nicaragua's answer to David Attenborough who was almost guaranteeing sloth sightings. He'd worked for shows like Survivor and for a low price could take us from San Juan to sloths for a couple of hours. We were sold and met him on Saturday afternoon. He took us through some of his neighbours' properties to some sparsely placed trees. On the way he whipped a porcupine out of a bush and passed it round for us to become acquainted with. About 40 minutes in, Señor Attenborough was peering through some leaves at a sleepy lump. It was a sloth. He woke her up and wrestled her down for us again to get acquainted with. She was really fast and viscous when she wanted to be but very cute. We placed her on the ground to get a good look her super long and strong arms and just as the rain started to pour from the sky we returned her to a tree and made a beeline back to San Juan del Sur. Mission accomplished.

Sunday funday rolled around in San Juan and, being the grandparents we are, we sought any excuse to escape our hostel, which had become drinking games central. We found solace in a zip lining tour in the jungle just outside town. Seven zip lines, a steep wooden stair case and a crazy guide awaited us. It was good fun zipping through the trees and gave us some great views of the town below. And as luck would have it we saw a sloth and several monkeys on the way into the park as we slid through the fresh mud.

Feeling we'd had our fix of partying backpackers we jumped in a taxi and then a ferry to the more sedate Ometepe Island–an island in the middle of Lake Nicaragua formed by two large volcanoes. We were met at the port by a tuk tuk that drove us 1km to our hosts, cows and chickens covered the road randomly while people hung their washing on barbed wired fences. We spent most of our time soaking up what seemed to be more authentic Nicaraguan life from the hosts home and then a rented scooter. Having wheels allowed us to lap the island, check out the views of the volcanoes and stop off for a quick swim in the natural spring. We also climbed part way up volcán Concepción, Adam made it further than Elle but both of us have never sweated so much in our lives. It was hard work and not very rewarding and clouds prevented us for seeing the views.

Next stop was the colonial gem of Nicaragua–Granada. It's the second biggest city in the country and has maintained a lot of character. Our host picked us up from the port and we spent the afternoon wandering the centre seeking out tours. Not long after our host offered to take us up to the newly active volcano Masaya to see the lava lake. It was incredible to see the lava bubbling beneath the rocks in slow motion, smoke billowing from the crater. It didn't look real and was so close to the road, with only a knee high stone wall to hold back the crowd, it would have to be illegal in any other part of the world.

After some relaxing wet weather days, we took a boat tour around the many tiny islands formed by the rocks spewed out by the Mombacho volcano 20,000 years ago. Many of the islands are privately owned by Nicaragua's richest families—like the one that owns the biggest brewery in the country—while many are still home to locals fishing and washing clothes in the lake. And one particularly special island is home to five monkeys rescued by a local vet. We bounced our way back across the choppy lake and prepared for our impending flight to Guatemala.
