Del mar a las montañas, Chile

Mar 2016 · 2016-03-10

On Saturday morning we took the two hour bus trip from Santiago, through the Casablanca valley wine region, to the coastal city of Valparaiso (val-para-ee-so).

Our initial impressions were that Valpo, as it is called by the locals, couldn't be more different from the clean and sophisticated Santiago. Getting off the bus the city centre felt grungey and rundown, not like the UNESCO world heritage site that we'd read about. Nevertheless we found a taxi to our hospedaje, a small B&B on one of the hills surrounding the town centre and followed our host, Ani's, advice on where to explore.

Turns out Valpo is really nice, heritage filled and "bohemian" as Ani kept saying. The houses are stacked all up the hillside as far as you can see and painted every different colour. We spent our days wandering the steep hills and looking at the old houses and graffiti, we also made it to Pablo Neruda's house (if we could just remember who he is). We even stumbled across a vegetarian restaurant that was visited by a musical duo (guitar and piano accordion, check out that link) offering to play songs...the old guy at the next table was loving it, and even joined in playing the spoons. Sunday is pretty quiet at the coast so we wandered down to the port area, found it to be dirty and then carried on walking up and down hills and using the fun, old outdoor elevators the locals use to conquer the hills.

On Monday we came back to Santiago for our tour to the Andes. Having just made the tour bus we started the two hour journey to the mountains from Santiago, stopping to collect more people occasionally. We eventually arrived at the bottom of the snow capped volcano and glacier that stretch to 6500m, pretty amazing. We'd only be trekking to about 2500m on our tour for an amazing view of it all in a flat plateau. We made it about 20m up when our Argentinian friend who'd probably had a few too many empanadas couldn't go on. Annoying as it was, it was fortuitous because we heard some cries from under a nearby rock. After some digging, our guide Christian freed a puppy that had fallen under some boulders, he tells us it's not something he sets up for each group but either way he looked like a hero. We named the puppy Rocky, gave him some food and he came to the top plateau with us. Luckily the trek wasn't too arduous and was followed by delicious lunch overlooking the river, glacier and snow covered volcano. The views were awesome although blighted by the obvious presence of mining, which Christian told us is being made worse by a private hydroelectric scheme to power the mines.

After a fast journey down and a reunion with Ms Argentina, the bus took us down a terribly bumpy dirt road to the Colima hot springs. Hot is the word, the top pool was 50 degrees, and the lower pools more like 35, much more pleasant. The views from the pools were similarly amazing but the smell of Sulfur and arsenic, not so much. The bus ride home was slow due to crazy peak hour traffic and then got impossible when we drove right into the middle of a protest about the proposed new divorce laws that will allow men to remarry straight away after divorcing, while women must wait 9 months...I'd protest that too but it added some stress to a long day.

We're off to Buenos Aires tomorrow night so just relaxing today and tomorrow. Stay tuned for the next adventure.
