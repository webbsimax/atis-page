Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

Apr 2016 · 2016-04-16

We'd read a lot about safety issues in Rio before we arrived so when we landed we were paranoid that every person was out to rob us...happily it turns out our fears were misplaced. Rio is a lovely city full of friendly people, that's not to say we weren't careful as we would be in any big city but it was not at all like the media suggested, thankfully. What is true is that the humidity was bad, everyone does wear Havaianas, coconut water and acai are popular and bronzed mostly naked Brazilians fill the beaches playing soccer and volleyball, surfing and swimming...and Brazil is expensive! It's pretty clear that the Rio government had spent some dollars fixing things up for the olympics—the metro was great and there were newly installed tourist friendly signs everywhere, and still a lot "under construction", hopefully to be finished just in time for the games.

We spent our first five days ticking off the must see items like Sugarloaf cable car at sunset, getting the train up to Christ the Redeemer, riding the tram to Escadaria Selarón, visiting Copacabana and the botanic gardens and touring the city centre. All of these were lovely and very much representative of the city and its culture. The view across the city from Sugarloaf mountain at sunset was a highlight— with Christ the redeemer at the top of the hill opposite looking down across the city.

A few days into our stay we took a long bus and boat ride to Ilha Grande, also known as paradise. Although it hasn't always been paradise, it was originally a leper colony, then a prison used to house political prisoners during the last military government. But since the mid 90s it's been a lovely island covered in jungle surrounded by beaches, with no cars and no ATMs. We relaxed on the beach and swam in the warm South Atlantic for a couple of days and ventured out by boat (the only way to get around, with no cars or roads) to find a more secluded beach called Lopes Mendes. The surf was a bit big to really make the most of the beach but it was still lovely, particularly when we found the calmer bayside beaches, with unbelievably warm water for the open ocean.

Finally we came back to Leblon in Rio for a few days, it's a very rich suburb near Ipanema beach and a giant lake, which we walked the entire way around. We watched the sun set over Ipanema beach, and visited a brand new museum called the museum of tomorrow, covering everything from the big bang through to population and climate change. On our way out a man accidentally walked straight off the edge and fell into a pool of water shopping in hand (hilarious—for us at least). Leblon was an insight into how the other half live but not somewhere we'd want to spend all of my time in Rio, especially given a third of the city's population lives in favelas—the shanty towns that surround the suburbs. We decided not to do any of the favela tours so can't comment on life there...poverty doesn't seem like something that should be turned into a tourist attraction!

From Leblon we headed to an airport hotel for a wonderfully early flight to Peru.
