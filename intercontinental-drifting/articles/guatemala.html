Guatemala

Jun 2016 · 2016-06-26

With time and safety concerns looming large we decided to splurge on a flight from Managua to Guatemala City. It was pricey but would mean that we could have a good amount of time in Mexico before our flight to Cuba and not have to endure some pretty gruelling bus rides. On reflection it was totally worth it, we jumped on the plane with about 10 others and were in Guatemala in record time and then onto a shuttle to Antigua a short time after that. Two days of travel saved.

In our time in Antigua we did a self guided walking tour of the city, tripping on the decaying cobblestones; bargained our way to ownership of some new party slippers and cushion covers; admired the colourful chicken buses and savoured some local produce.

We were awoken on our first morning by the room shaking and the ceiling light swinging—our first experience of an earthquake. While the 5.2 quake felt strong to us, we glanced out the window to see the hostel staff barely break step. Later enquires revealed that earthquakes are an almost weekly occurrence in this part of the world.

Our visit to Antigua was a short one but nice all the same, and a repeat for Elle. On our last day, with some time to kill in Antigua before our overnight bus to Flores, we did a tour of all the parks in the city enjoying the people watching and blue skies....and then shamefully, for the first time this trip, we devoured some fast food, namely McDonald's. To be fair Antigua McDonald's is no ordinary McDonald's. It's a sprawling colonial building complete with huge internal courtyard and fountains and a McCafe. While we were waiting the heavens opened and we were glad to be inside enjoying free wifi and familiar tastes (and dessert table service, take note McDonald's Australia).

In true Guatemalan style, that night we sped dangerously quickly through the streets and onto the highway and just made our 9pm bus to Flores. Eight hours of first class luxury (well, for a bus) awaited...and then flew by. We arrived at 5 am, way too early to be banging on our host's door so we waited it out with a guy at his coffee stand. At 7 am a tuk tuk ride had never seemed more appealing and we made it to our digs. Later we wandered around the island of Flores, in the middle of Lake Petén Itzá. There's not much to the island so we'd circumnavigated it in about 20 minutes soaking up the sun and wondering where all the locals were. We made it back to our hosts' in time for dinner—a hearty mix of traditional edible flowers ...it was interesting and tasty, and definitely Guatemalan.

The next day we headed to Tikal with our host Marlon who is also a tour guide at the ruins. Tikal was one of the largest cities in the Mayan civilisation, thought to have been home to as many as 900,000 people between 200AD and 900AD. Today the ruins of many of the temples and palaces remain, and are still in the process of being uncovered from the jungle that has surrounded them.

We headed out early to avoid the midday heat and humidity, and spent the morning exploring the pyramid temples, palaces and plazas, all separated by dense jungle. It was amazing to be able to sit on top of one of the temples, and see the tops of the other pyramids poking up above the jungle canopy. We also saw toucans, monkeys and an agouti as we headed between ruins, possibly seeing more wildlife here than we did on the special wildlife tours we had done previously.

Before we knew it, it was time to leave Guatemala and head into the Mexican Yucatan peninsula via Belize on yet another extended bus ride. Time to visit Elle's country of choice, and for Adam to see how many tacos he can eat.
