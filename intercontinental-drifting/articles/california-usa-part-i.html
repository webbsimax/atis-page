California, USA part I

Aug 2016 · 2016-08-02

After our brief jaunt to Texas, we boarded a flight westbound to California and picked up a borrowed car. We immediately fought our way out of LAX and onto Warner Springs—a small town in the Peninsula Ranges, about 100km west of San Diego. Warner Springs is home to a busy gliderport, some of Adam's pilot friends, a golf course....and not much else. We spent a few fun days at the gliderport flying, dune buggying, relaxing and planning our upcoming road trip to San Francisco, Yosemite and Death Valley.

Having travelled for four months with the same clothes, we decided it was time to hit up the outlets, so we drove from Warner Springs towards the Mexico USA border. We took the last exit before the border and found the mall we were after, you could see the giant Mexican flag and two meter wall marking the beginning of Mexico and the end of the USA. We bought a few items and had a few eye opening American experiences, including bargain shop chaos. Satisfied with our purchases we headed back to our base in the ranges.

The next day we headed out again toward San Diego, with separate plans for the day. Adam visited the USS Midway museum, while Elle sought a much needed haircut. The Midway was a US aircraft carrier built in 1945, and in service until the early 1990s. It is now a floating museum, complete with a huge collection of aircraft, and lots of information on flying from the aircraft carrier. It was heaven for Adam. After we both finished exploring, we met up again for a quick city tour before driving home again. San Diego was playing host to Comicon at the time so there was a lot happening but it was nice to have a quick walk around the historic downtown gas lamp district.

Finally, it was time to leave the gliderport and start road tripping north in our car, kindly lent to us by Garret who's family we'd been staying with at the gliderport—first stop Los Angeles. Not being movie buffs, or keen for bus tours of celebrities' houses, LA wasn't really our kind of city so we kept it brief. We spent our first night up at the Griffith Observatory to watch the sunset over the Hollywood sign. The next morning we battled the notorious LA traffic to check out Venice beach and Santa Monica. We walked the promenade, checked out all the weird happenings and then stopped for some food truck pizza. It was a quick stop but we fitted enough in to move on to San Francisco, heading north up the coastal Highway One.
