Mexico City, Mexico

Jul 2016 · 2016-07-22

Our (very Mexican) adventures in México started pretty much immediately after we landed from Cuba. We jumped straight into an airport taxi, checked into our Airbnb and then ran to the Lucha Libre wrestling to meet Brooke and Kath and several of their friends. The wrestling was good fun, plenty of beers, street tacos, drama and profanity to be had. We saw some one on one fights, a female fight and a couple of team battles, including one featuring a muscular gentleman with nothing but gold speedos on, a hit with the females in the crowd. It was a welcome reprieve after our time in Cuba.

After that we passed our time in Mexico at a more leisurely pace, ticking off all the main sights little by little. We caught up with Brooke and Kath again one day to explore the massive urban forest of Chapultepec, seeking out an unusual exhibition on pre-WWII Japanese playgrounds, as well as stopping by for the traditional performance by the voladores, Mexico's answer to the maypole, where men swing through the air from the top of a pole, attached only by a rope tied to their waist.

In our next days we headed over to the Centro Histórico to admire one of the biggest outdoor public plazas in the world along with the national palace, anthropology museum, markets, remnants of Tenochtitlan's Templo Mayor, Bellas Artes, Torre Latino and the very empty mariachi square. All of it was very interesting and unique.

When not touristing, we just enjoyed wandering Roma, the hipster neighbourhood we were staying in. We found ourselves eating and coffeeing our way across the suburb and strolling into upscale Condesa to hang out at the plazas and parks. Elle even managed to join in on the street  food at a vegan taco stand in Roma. Soy meat tacos al pastor with potato and cactus were pretty unique in a country that lives to eat animal. Adam had plenty of opportunity to try the local meaty offerings, sold on every street corner. Favourites include tacos al pastor, a kind of pork donner kebab with pineapple, usually sold for around 5 pesos (40 cents) each.

On the Saturday, we ventured out a little further on the metrobus to what used to be a separate town before being engulfed by Mexico City-the San Angel market. We walked around the plaza and then to Frida and Diego's (some Mexican artists) stomping ground in beautiful Coayacan. It was a relaxing taste of the quiter Mexican life away from the busy central suburbs of the city. It was the weekend so everyone was out in the main plaza enjoying the sun and the churros.

As always, time flew by and we quickly found ourselves on the way to the airport for another flight to another country but with a promise to return to one of the best countries on earth. That's it for the Spanish speaking leg of our journey it's all English speaking for the next few months... first up Texas!
