Bogota, Colombia

Jun 2016 · 2016-06-01

We officially finished the south American leg of our trip in Colombia—another country that gets an undeserved bad rap. We had just one week in the capital Bogota but managed to fit in a lot and, while we were always careful, we had no safety issues especially not in the relatively safe and predominantly gay neighbourhood of Chapinero where we stayed.

Our first few days we spent getting to know the old city called La Candelaria. It's a heritage laden neighbourhood that's a bit rough in parts but has a lot to see. We enjoyed a walking tour, which included some local moonshine and the Botero art museum then lunched with some other travellers. In the days that followed we covered the country's important gold Museum and saw the city from up high on the hill of Montserrate.

The next day we did what is one of the highlights of the trip so far and visited some coffee farms a couple of hours outside of Bogota. We started in a bigger town at the coffee buyers who purchase beans from farmers and sell them onto Bogota based companies for exportation. Apparently this year's crop isn't the best because of the drought so it's only worth a couple of dollars per kilo.

From there we visited a couple of family coffee farms. We met an awesome farmer, Miguel, who showed us his crops (while his coffee had all been harvested, he proudly showed us his avocado and banana trees, as well as various fruits we hadn't heard of), and he gave us a tour of his equipment and processes. He then made us some delicious coffee from some of his plants. Later we were put to work on another family farm, completing the whole coffee process from picking the 'cherries' to roasting and griding. We got to spend sometime with the family inbetween processes, which was really fun. In the end we were able to buy some family grown coffee for about $5 a pack before returning to Bogota. You can watch a video of us on the tour here.

After some locally brewed Bogota beer and before we knew it, Sunday had rolled around. On Sunday mornings Bogota's main streets close and turn into cycle paths for 'ciclovia', so we took advantage and walked 6km to Usaquen for the flea market. There wasn't a lot we hadn't already seen but we did enjoy some artisan chorizo and home made cake.

On our last day we went to what we now know is a bit of a tacky tourist trap... The salt cathedral (I.e a salt mine turned tourist attraction thanks to some clever marketing). We tackled the packed transmilenio metro bus out of Bogota to start the journey to the Cathedral, changed buses and arrived to the rough looking town of Zipaquirá. Unfortunately there wasn't a lot of interest to us at the cathedral, various crosses and Chapels carved into one part of a working salt mine. To be honest we weren't terribly impressed and headed back to Bogota as soon as possible.

And so ended our time in wonderful South America, next up Central America with much less of a plan than before.
