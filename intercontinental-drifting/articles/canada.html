Canada

Aug 2016 · 2016-08-24

Landing in Vancouver was like arriving home in a way. Everything seemed familiar—its a lot less chaotic than America, more shops are on the street rather than in huge complexes, people are relaxed and the eating scene is not dominated by chain restaurants. We stayed with a lovely couple by the "beach" (not so much like home) in Kitsilano and enjoyed soaking up the relaxed outdoor atmosphere. On day one, we satisfied our hankering for quality cheese (something Canada seems to do badly and expensively) by hitting up the Granville Island market and picnicking the afternoon away with nine year aged cheddar. The next day we ventured to downtown for some sightseeing and eating, and on the third day we rented bikes and completed a lap of the beautiful Stanley Park, which has a view of the harbour, typical pine trees and a big lake complete with beavers. Vancouver is a low key city and one we imagine being amazing in the winter.

A quick cross-country journey later we arrived in Toronto. At this point for us one big city is starting to blur into another so forgive us if we seem unenthused by Toronto. It does have some interesting sites like the CN tower, hockey hall of fame and Union station, which we spent a day taking a tour of via the network of underground walkways (important when it's -20 outside). The second and only other day we had was meant to be spent learning about baseball at a Blue Jays game, but that wasn't to be (apparently much of Canada had that same idea). We found ourselves a microbrewery and some fondue and spent the afternoon out of the rain.

On local advice that the train between Toronto and Montreal was nice, we booked our tickets and jumped aboard. We managed to disembark right in the of the pride parade, which was a colourful welcome to Montreal. Montreal is really French (a lot more than expected) and mostly about eating and drinking, which we did plenty of—sampling bagels, coffee, Poutine (chips, cheese and gravy), butter and Portuguese tarts and smoked meat. Delicious. We did find some time to go ice skating (some more successful than others), wander around the old port, plateau and downtown, as well as an evening of in-park drinking and eating with a friend of Adam's.

Sitting down to write this post, it struck us that we didn't have a lot to say, not that we didn't enjoy Canada, we did. We just spent out time eating and getting to know the cities rather than checking off tourist sites. It was nice and refreshing, and we'd like to return in the winter to some less built up areas.
