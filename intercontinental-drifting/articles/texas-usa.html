Texas, USA

Jul 2016 · 2016-07-27

Arriving in America, and in particular Texas, was a bigger culture shock than any of the other dozen or so countries we've visited in the past few months. They say everything is bigger in Texas, and it really is. Bigger roads, bigger houses, bigger personalities, bigger portion sizes and bigger cars. We fitted right in with our hired pick up truck (ute)–a free upgrade from the tiny Fiat we booked–that is until we needed to refuel it and had no idea how to.

Overcoming slow airport security, gas station embarrassment and a series of confusing 'spaghetti roads' (huge winding meeting points between highways) we were on our way to the small town of Waxahachie, an hour or so outside of Dallas. We'd aranged to stay with a local family and then meet up with some of Adam's gliding friends at the Texas soaring centre, where the first ever US junior national championships were being held. Unfortunately we didn't have a lot of time to spend in Waxahachie but from what we saw it is a really pretty, small, old town, more American than we'd imagined with flags and Texas state decorations proudly displayed outside most houses.

The next day, we continued our quest south to the capital, Austin, for a few days. Given Texas was an unintended stop over, we decided to use our time there to get to know one of the most hipster places in the USA—an especially strange place given the conservative government that's housed there. Austin was definitely hipster (and hot). We were greeted by friendly left wing people, riding single speed bikes, sipping lattes and ordering breakfast tacos from food trucks. We had a lot of fun exploring the downtown area, the food truck parks and the Capitol building, and meeting up with a friend of Elle's from Mexico for beers at one of the hundreds of watering holes in the city.

On our other free day we escaped the heat with everyone else in the city at Barton Springs— a cold, deep underground water spring just near downtown. The water was freezing (and a little algae covered) but it was a nice way to cool down after the 40 degree days we'd endured. After a quick swim we found ourselves lunching on burgers and then heading out to a bar that was part saloon, part hipster hang out. Another interesting American experience.

Feeling satisfied by the copious fast food we ate and the small taste of Austin we got to experience, we drove back to Dallas for our flight to Los Angeles. We got the sense that we'd need to return to Austin to fully understand its weird ways—another place to add to the return list.
