Yucatan, Mexico

Jul 2016 · 2016-07-07

Two border crossings, two buses and 12 hours after we'd left Flores Guatemala, we finally landed in Chetumal Mexico. We had no further plans til our flight to Cuba so decided to stay put for a night to come up with a plan for our week in the Yucatan. It didn't take long to decide to spend a few days relaxing by the beautiful freshwater lagoon in Bacalar—a sleepy town 45 minutes from Chetumal. The lagoon is 42km long and is several spectacular shades of blue and green, due to its white limestone bottom. Our accommodation was right on the water, complete with sun beds, hammocks and a private jetty for easy access to the warm waters.

The next day we grabbed a cab to something we'd not heard of before—a cenote. It's a sink hole filled with fresh underground water...apparently the Yucatan is full of them. Cenote Azule, which we visited, was very pretty and exceptionally deep (90m), thankfully a rope was strung across to provide some relief from dog paddling. Apparently it's a special cenote because it's out in the open and not buried in a cave like many others. The swim plus some cenote side drinks provided welcome relief from the extreme heat and humidity.

After a few days it was time to move on to another slice of paradise—Tulum. Tulum is famous for its beachside Mayan ruins, and for being a better alternative to the American filled party centres of Playa del Carmen and Cancun. The town itself is very small and very hot. We quickly rented some bikes, bought some tacos and cheap beers and set about exploring.

We spent a day checking out the Tulum ruins on bike. The ruins are in an incredible spot, right on the cliffs overlooking the white sand and turquoise water of the Caribbean coast, palm trees and iguanas dotted around them. Tulum was one of the last cities built and inhabited by the Maya people and remained occupied 70 years after the Spanish conquest. It's only a small site so we finished up quickly and headed for the beach, grey storm clouds threatening overhead. The moment we touched the sand, water started pouring from the sky, we were soaked and still had to ride back to our accommodation, without a swim in the beautiful ocean.

Despite the heavy rain and a vague warning of a potential cyclone, we decided to tick off another bucket list item—swimming with whale sharks, the largest fish in the sea (and neither whales nor sharks). We chucked all our things in the back of a mini van and headed toward Cancun to grab a boat out to the whale sharks' feeding grounds in the open ocean. Once on the water and out of sight of land we found ourselves circling for an eternity before we eventually joined some other boats that had spotted a whale shark. We excitedly jumped in to take a swim with it, but snorkel, mask and timing issues meant it wasn't our most successful swimming session. Luckily we had two more sessions to come, after some more waiting. We circled some more hoping for the sun to shine and bring plankton and whale sharks to the surface. Eventually we got the opportunity to swim very close to two 7-8m whale sharks. Putting aside our ethical concerns, it was a really incredible experience—they're amazing creatures.

After the excitement and sun burn of the whale sharks, we spent our last few days in Cancun relaxing, including a trip to Cancun beach—one of the most beautiful in the world—unfortunately ruined by the towering hotels stretching as far as you can see. We also spent time preparing for our next leg to Cuba—in particular trying to take money out of an Australian bank account in Mexico to change into Euros so we had something usable in Cuba...fun times.
