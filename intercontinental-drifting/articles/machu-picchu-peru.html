Machu Picchu, Peru

May 2016 · 2016-05-08

When we arrived to Cusco we wondered if we might regret our decision to take a two day trek to Machu Picchu, rather than the full four day Inca trail. After one day, 16km of hiking and a glimpse at the toilet situation on the trail, we knew we'd made the right decision! We had a hotel bed, flushing toilet and hot shower to look forward to after our hike.

We had three nights in Cusco before our trek to acclimatise to the 3,399m (11,150') altitude... We needed it. Everything is harder that high up and the steps up to our hostel on a hill overlooking the city felt like climbing a mountain. Cusco is a beautiful little city, loads of old architecture, cobblestone streets and plenty of culture. We wandered the plazas and enjoyed the views from our hostel, all the while sipping coca tea to alleviate altitude sickness.

On our last night in Cusco we bought the most amazing traditional hand beaded and hand sewed hat off a guy in the street, not without drama. We first saw him in an alley with other vendors but all of sudden he was packing up and running. The police had arrived. We wandered some streets and found him again and we managed to negotiate a good price for what will be an excellent decoration for our home.. Wherever that will be.

The day before our trek we had a briefing with our guide Luís and met the other people coming with us, we really lucked out and got a lovely Australian couple. (We saw many undesirable trekkers on our travels so felt very happy with our group.) We were all collected at 5am the next day and off we went toward Machu Picchu. The two day trek starts part way down the train line that goes to Machu Picchu pueblo (aka Aguascalientes) and joins up with the last day of the full Inca trail. About 20 minutes out from Aguascalientes we jumped off the train in the middle of the tracks and made our first of many steps up to the ruins.

It was a long day of trekking up steep stairs and along narrow paths, the trail is tough and we were not even on the hardest part. We have complete respect for the many porters on the trail who can easily complete all four days carrying 25kg packs filled to the brim with gas bottles, camping gear and tourists' belongings. In fact the record time for the annual porter race is somewhere around four hours to complete the 45km Inca trail—that's the one that takes the average tourist four days! We were no match for the porters but Luis was surprised at how quickly the group was moving—we think he was keen for a break, having completed two full trails in the preceding days—so we stopped for lunch just next to Winawayna, an amazing set of terraces and ruins toward the end of the Inca trail.

An hour or so later of uphill trekking we reached the sun gate, Machu Picchu was just visible in the distance perched on top of the mountains. The view was amazing and showed us what an incredible spot the Incans had chosen to build the town. Many photos and a slow decent later we got a close up look at Machu Picchu, it was perfect weather and it all looked pretty surreal, especially with llamas grazing in the foreground.

We weren't to know how lucky we had been to see everything in the sunlight until the next day when we got up at 5am for a sunrise that never eventuated. The notorious cloud forest was covering Machu Picchu in thick mist and drizzly rain. Luís did his best to keep us positive and showed us around the impressive ruins. We learned how the Incans had cleverly constructed windows to frame the sun during the solstice, carved water pools to watch the stars and configured water fountains to deal with rain and irrigate the many crops growing in the microclimates created by the vast terraces along the agricultural section of Machu Picchu.

After the tour we had some free time to explore and thankfully things started to clear a little, at least to the point that we could make out the ruins. We walked up to the Inca bridge and took about 400 more photos before meeting back with the group. With that our Machu Picchu experience came to a close. We said goodbye to our new Australian friends and our guide and headed back to the comfort of our hotel for one last night before catching the train back to Cusco.
