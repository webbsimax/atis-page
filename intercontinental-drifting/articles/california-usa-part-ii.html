California, USA part II

Aug 2016 · 2016-08-10

Part two of our USA road trip saw us continue our northward drive up the California coast to San Francisco via Atascadero. It was a beautiful drive on highway one but there was so much fog that a lot of the view was obscured all the way to San Francisco. That said, we did manage to stop and check out the colony of elephant seals at San Simeon on the way through. They were huge and pretty interesting, fighting each other and lazing on the beach, but super stinky. After another brief unscheduled stop near Big Sur while we waited for a tree to be moved off the winding road, it was all plain sailing to San Francisco.

We got to San Francisco quite late so we dumped our stuff and parked the car in a garage where it would stay until we left the city again. The next two days we set out to explore the highlights of what we could confirm was the fog city (and freezing city even in summer and even when it's 40 degrees in nearby towns) via the public transport system. San Francisco is a cool place, and very much how we'd imagined—old houses lined up along hilly streets with old cable cars crawling passed. We headed out to the infamous Golden Gate Bridge only to find it almost completely shrouded in fog. After some waiting we were rewarded with a brief glimpse of the bridge and a view across the bay as the fog cleared for a few seconds, albeit slightly obscured. We then made our way to Lombard St to walk up the switchbacks and admire the city from above. On our second day we took a walking tour of downtown and Chinatown, stopping at Coit Tower and Pier 39 to get to know the local sea lion population (just like Tony Hawk). We had just enough time to do our laundry self-service style and grab some Burritos before packing up. There's a lot we didn't see so it's another place to drop by again sometime.

As per our vague schedule we cruised into Merced, our Yosemite base, very quickly and easily from San Francisco. It was a two hour drive into the Yosemite Valley from Merced but it was totally worth it. We spent two full days in the park admiring all of its natural wonders. On the first day we were lucky to avoid the weekend, summer crowd and drove to El Capitan, Glacier Point, Tunnel View and Swinging Bridge, all of which provided some pretty spectacular views and opportunities for happy snaps (very much like the screensaver photos you see). The next day we weren't so lucky and the park swelled with Saturday adventurers. But it wasn't so bad taking the shuttle up to the Yosemite falls trailhead where we did a short, easy hike up to the lower falls where we ate our packed lunch. We enjoyed the magnificent vista on some rocks to the side of the falls, the spray from the water cooling us down. The heat at Yosemite was pretty strong, but it would be nothing compared to our next stop—Death Valley.

We chose the small town of Lone Pine as our base for the intimidating Death Valley. It's a cute little place resembling a movie set, mostly because it has played host to many film crews over time. From Lone Pine we drove about two hours into the visitors centre within Death Valley (Elle slightly panicked about the car breaking down the whole way). It was hot! Our phone recorded 52 degrees Celsius and the National Parks thermometer read 121 degrees Fahrenheit. The heat made any on-foot adventures difficult but we did get out at Badwater salt flats and walk a regrettable 500m or so. From there we explored (in the air-conditioned car) artist's palette—a series of rocks stained with various, natural colours; Mesquite sand dunes; and the old borax works. The scenery was pretty amazing, very sparse and unique, like the moon only hotter. It would've been great to get to some ghost towns but their isolation and the unbearable heat meant that was impossible. Staying on the well-populated tourist road was terrifying enough!

Having spent long enough in the unforgiving desert we made a hasty exit, destination Warner Springs to return the car and ready ourselves for our next leg of the trip in Canada. As we probably should've predicted, we didn't quite spend our time in Warner Springs doing tax returns, washing and other life admin as planned. We got a bit extreme and went wake boarding in Canyon Lake with some friends of Adam's—some of us wake boarded while others belly boarded but we won't go into specifics. And mid-tax return Adam received an invitation he couldn't refuse to go gliding. Our final day was spent completing a few more American must-dos, heading out for a traditional "biscuits and gravy" (containing neither biscuits, nor gravy) for breakfast, and Taco Bell for lunch. All in all a fun few days before heading out to San Diego for a flight to Vancouver.
