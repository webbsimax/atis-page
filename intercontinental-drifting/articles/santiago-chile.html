Santiago, Chile

Mar 2016 · 2016-03-05

On Monday we left Sydney for the first of our adventures overseas, 12 hours later we were in Santiago, Chile. After a AU$70 (!) taxi ride we quickly learnt that Chile was not cheap and that mistakes would be costly. We were clearly very ill informed or ill prepared, because Chile isn't what we expected. It's a lot more expensive and developed than imagined. The cost of living and average salary are on par with Australia and the infrastructure (the metro is great) makes the city seem like a European centre, in the best possible way. Even the street dogs—of which there are hundreds—seem relatively healthy (you can see a video we took of a street dog attacking a sprinkler here). We have noticed some interesting and probably superfluous employment going on too—individual workers watering the grass with a hose and someone solely to weigh your fruit at the supermarket. We gather it's better to have people in jobs, whatever they are, than to have them rely on welfare—either way we're impressed by Chile.

Finding vegetarian food, or any food in general, has been interesting. A lot of Chile's food joints offer hot dogs, fried empanadas and various kinds of meat. Needless to say we've eaten a lot of cheese and avocado, and oil. Hopefully this improves along with our Spanish.

What we have been up to:


We spent our first few days exploring the city. On Tuesday, walked over to Cerro Negro and took the funicular (like a tram that goes up at 45 degrees) to the top to see the giant Mary statue and city-wide views. (We are sure the views are amazing from there when there isn't a blanket of smog covering everything.) It was a really impressive bit of infrastructure too, mountain bike tracks and toilets and BBQs. Coming down that hill was an adventure through dirt tracks and bush and brought us out in a really fancy neighbourhood that could be mistaken for American suburbia. We had lunch in a park here and joined the hundreds of canoodling couples and street dogs.

Day two saw us spend the morning at the new museum of human rights. We learnt a lot about the Pinochet government and his impact on Chile, something we knew little of before coming here. The museum was really well put together and looked at truth and reconciliation–always interesting to an ex-RA employee! That afternoon, we took some time to check out the old and crumbling Brazil neighbourhood and got some Chilean-Mexican lunch. Then we headed to the major fruit and veg market to test out our Spanish. There were loads of delicious fresh things and amazing, cheap avocados. We managed to leave with most of the things we needed for dinner, despite some confusion between bread crumbs and bread rolls at the panaderia.

Yesterday, we headed out to try the 'second-best' empanadas in Chile near the central market. We burnt off the calories checking out the Plaza de Armas, an ex military training ground and now a town plaza where Chileans hang out, and then the national palace, Palacio Moneda. The palace was bombed in the 70s when Pinochet staged a coup, but you wouldn't know it. Today it is immaculate, and houses an enormous Chilean flag. We topped the day off with a funny dinner at a Chilean restaurant, with a very literal menu. Yes, the hamburger with avocado is just meat and (lots of) avocado, and the cheese sandwich, just (lots of) cheese.

Next stops:


Valparaiso on the Chilean coast and a volcano and hot springs tour at Cajon del Maipo in the Chilean Andes, then to Buenos Aires, Argentina.
