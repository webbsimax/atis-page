Ecuador

May 2016 · 2016-05-20

Not long after we touched down in Quito we were in a homestay with a family that runs a Spanish school from their backyard. They had a huge home and two lovely dogs, which were good company given we were the only students at the school. It seems loads of tourist cancelled their trips to Ecuador following news of the earthquake, it's a shame because Quito is totally fine and the country could use the tourist dollars.

We spent four days in one-on-one private classes in attempt to brush up our skills and learned a lot about Ecuadorian culture, history and politics in our time—including the very big difference between rich and poor Ecuadorians and various thoughts on the current socialist government. On our last day our two teachers took us on an excursion to the historic centre where visited a load of churches and tried cheesy hot chocolate, weird flavour ice cream (think blue cheese and rice flavour—surprisingly tasty) and some Ecuadorian moonshine that kept everyone very happy all afternoon.

After we left the homestay we decided to continue Spanish lessons with the private teachers away from the school. This meant braving the daunting public transport system, which we managed to do quite easily and for only 25c each. We learned a lot from one surprisingly liberal teacher including how to procure a male stripper in a male dominated, Catholic society; how to officially give a child an English name in Ecuador; how the introduction of the US dollar in Ecuador hasn't benefited everyone and how to cure all manner of black magic curses using guinea pigs and turtle blood ...all very interesting.

Inbetween our Spanish classes we were desperately trying to be tourists, as well as set up a tour to the Galápagos. The first of those tasks was more successful than the second. We managed to get up the cable car to the volcano overlooking Quito, it was freezing at over 12,000ft but the view was pretty impressive—Quito is surrounded by volcanoes. We then headed out to "mitad del mundo", or the middle of the world (equator), to see both the official and not so official Equator. We saw some dubious experiments involving eggs, flushing water and line walking. We're not convinced, especially given two different places claim to be the actual equator, our phone said neither was 00°00'00"N, and in reality the equator is 5km wide...we enjoyed being in the middle of the world nonetheless.

We eventually accepted that $5000 for five days cruising in the Galápagos was a little much at this early stage of our trip, and sought out alternative adventures. We found just what we were looking for in Baños, an adventure capital a few hours from Quito—yes your dictionary will translate the name as toilets but it's named after the thermal pools near the city.

We had limited time in Baños but made the most of it by jumping on an early "express" bus (it stopped whenever a local flagged it down, which was all the time along the three hour route) and arriving by lunchtime. We did a quick lap of the town and then booked ourselves on an afternoon white water rafting tour. This was one of the best activities we've done so far. We had an awesome time in the rapids, despite capsizing several times and an alleged near drowning incident, and met some other people on similar trips.

With no time to waste we went off to the Puyo jungle the next day. Puyo is part of the Amazon, known as the "secondary jungle"—the primary jungle requires days of travelling by planes, 4x4s and canoe to reach so we'll save that for next time. Our whirlwind trip included a visit to an animal sanctuary; traditional and terrifying canoe ride; trek to two waterfalls, one of which required swimming down a river to reach; overnight stay in a jungle hut; encounter with an Indigenous community and an impromptu chocolate making lesson (from the tree to a tasty hot chocolate) at a small family cacao farm. We had a great time getting to be at one with nature (sometimes a little too at one for Elle) and were lucky with the weather, it rained one day and sunny the other. In no time we we're back on the express bus and back in Quito with a flight to Bogota awaiting the next day.
