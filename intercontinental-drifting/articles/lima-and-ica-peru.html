Lima and Ica, Peru

Apr 2016 · 2016-04-28

Lima

How to put it nicely... Lima is a lovely city but it's more generic business centre than spectacular South American metropolis. The traffic is mental, architecture less impressive than the previous cities we have visited and tourist attractions less appealing. That said we enjoyed our time in the city, particularly by the sea front in the upscale neighbourhood of Miraflores. In our first Lima stint we squeezed in a city centre tour, a visit to a pre-Incan ruin, a wander around a strange water fountain park and some shopping at the handicraft market before the famous Lima fog rolled in and we were ready for a new city.

Ica

We found the break from the crazy traffic and symphony of car alarms we were seeking in Ica—a grungey city in the centre of one of the driest deserts on earth. We stayed with a lovely Airbnb family complete with pool, tortoise and McCaw parrot...and in our three days there managed to use at least three of our lives—we'll need to buy some more soon!

On our first day in Ica we ventured to Huacachina, a small oasis surrounded by huge sand dunes. We boarded a death trap sand buggie, sand boards in hand, and took off at great speed toward the giant dunes. We skidded and jumped across the dunes like a rickety roller coaster, stopping only occasionally to grab a board and zoom face first down the dunes on the boards. It was here we used a few of our lives and I was extremely thankfully when we stopped to watch the bright orange sun setting over the desert and then headed back for home. We'd survived day one.

Not content with our action fix in Ica, we spent the next day in Nazca to check out the "mysterious and enigmatic" Nazca lines—a collection of massive geoglyphs carved into the desert by the Nazca people and only visible from the air. Naturally, we jumped into a tiny plane and passed over the lines, the pilot throwing the plane left and right for the best views. Pretty incredible stuff. We were then quickly whisked away to some ruins of a Nazcan community that was eventually taken over by the Incans, who used a series of workers stationed across the land to relay messages and food and ultimately maintain their dominance. Finally, we visited the amazing engineering feat that is the Nazca aqueducts—a series of 18 swirly aqueducts carrying water through the desert thought to have been built in 540AD that are still used today. Nazcan and Incan people were incredibly intelligent!

We wanted our last day to be a little more relaxed so we arranged to visit some of the famous Pisco (grape based spirit) bodegas and vineyards around Ica. The father of our host family drove us to four very different bodegas ranging from a huge commercial entity to a tiny family distillery. We saw some pretty interesting techniques and drank more than enough sweet wine and Pisco. It's still not a drink we'd choose to drink too often.

After Ica we needed a rest so we're happy to come back to Lima and enjoy staying on the malecon next to the beach lined coast. Next up is the big one, what the trip was based around...Machu Picchu!
