Washington DC and New York, USA

Sep 2016 · 2016-09-03

Washington DC was very different to how we'd imagined and seen on TV (admittedly our best reference was sitcom Parks and Recreation, renowned for its factual accuracy we're sure). On arrival we found a strange mix of people, some basking in high-power, well-paid political jobs, and others who are genuinely struggling to get by in a pricey, busy city. We stayed in a quickly gentrifying area, which likely showed up this dichotomy more so than other parts, it was a very interesting insight into demographics, race relations and real life in this part of America.

DC has a lot to offer for free and we made the most of it. Our first day included plenty of walking as we wandered the National Mall, taking in the Capitol Building, Washington Monument, Lincoln Memorial, Luther King Memorial and the Whitehouse. The following days we enjoyed a few of the many museums that DC has to offer, including the American Holocaust museum, American Indian museum and the Air and Space museum. We found out the hard way that much of the good aerospace stuff is actually at another centre in neighbouring Virginia. Cue a two hour one-way trek on two trains and a bus to the Udvar-Hazy center. Luckily, it was totally worth the public transport pain to see the Smithsonian's pretty impressive collection of planes, gliders, and space stuff, like the Space Shuttle Discovery, Concorde, Enola Gay (the plane to drop the first atomic bomb) and lots more—over 250 aircraft—Adam was in heaven.

Our museum quota filled for a while, we jumped on a coach to New York City for our last week in North America. We started our New York stint with a day in Central Park, walking the paths, lazing in the sun and exploring the green patches. Sailing past the Statue of Liberty on the Staten Island Ferry, the 9/11 Memorial Museum and delicious pizza came next and gave us a full on day of touristing. Then we topped things off with a walk over the Brooklyn Bridge, brief walk through Times Square, shopping and catching up with a friend of Elle's. All enjoyable.

Having ticked bagels, pretzels, burgers, spaghetti and meatballs, pizza and donuts off our food to try list, we felt as though our cholesterol levels weren't yet high enough...so we took ourselves off for an afternoon of full on 'Merica, involving baseball and fries and hot dogs covered in cheese, ketchup and mustard. A delicious afternoon was had but unfortunately, it really wasn't the best game of the season—it turned out to be a dead rubber, crowd attendance was low, the Yankees didn't score a single point all game, and it was four innings before anyone actually hit a ball (crickets and tumbleweeds)...thrilling stuff, but worthwhile nonetheless. Our insides are thankfully that we jumped on a plane to Iceland and toward some hopefully healthier food.
